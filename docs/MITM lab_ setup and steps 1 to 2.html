  Why Trudy is wired, not Wi-Fi
  Two earlier plans turned out to be weaker than this one. Bridging Trudy's VM straight to the laptop's Wi-Fi card often fails silently, because the Wi-Fi driver and the access point drop frames carrying a second MAC address. Running Alice as a second VM on that same Wi-Fi card is no better, since one shared radio generally can't relay client-to-client traffic reliably. The fix: plug a USB-to-Ethernet adapter into the dev laptop, wire it straight into a TotoLink LAN port, and bridge Trudy's VM network adapter to that adapter in VMware. Trudy now has a real wired NIC, Alice and Bob stay native on Wi-Fi, and the bridge is reliable. If it still won't bridge cleanly, fall back to Kali booted from a USB stick on bare metal instead of a VM.

Step 2: eavesdropping on plain text


Goal: Trudy sits between Alice and Bob without touching any message, and reads the whole conversation in Wireshark. Nothing is changed in the chat app. Trudy only forwards traffic and watches it.




  How it works
  ARP spoofing tells Alice that Bob's IP belongs to Trudy's MAC address, and tells Bob the same about Alice. Their packets then arrive at Trudy, who forwards them on herself — ettercap does this internally, so the kernel's ip_forward should stay off (see the correction in the next step). Neither Alice nor Bob sees a change.

    Start ettercap (handles ARP spoofing and forwarding itself)
    
    
    Correction, 2026-11-20: earlier drafts of this step used two separate arpspoof terminals plus a manual ip_forward=1. Switched to ettercap instead — one command poisons both directions, and it forwards packets internally. Leave the kernel's ip_forward off for this — turning it on makes the kernel double-forward every packet alongside ettercap's own forwarding, a real documented ettercap gotcha that looks like traffic corruption. If it's already 1 from an earlier session: sudo sysctl -w net.ipv4.ip_forward=0.

What is next


Steps 3 and 4 go in the same environment. Step 3 adds AES and HMAC, after which Trudy's Wireshark shows only Base64, and her tampering is rejected. Step 4 is the active attack, where Trudy relays messages through her own Java program and can change or fabricate them. That is the only step where she has to run Trudy.java instead of Wireshark.
